# RDL 8/2026 Explained: Spain's 2% Rent Cap and 2-Year Extension Law

Spain's rental market is facing significant changes with the approval of Real Decreto-ley 8/2026, published in the Official State Gazette (BOE) on March 21, 2026 and effective from March 22, 2026.

## What RDL 8/2026 Actually Does

According to the BOE publication and legal analyses, RDL 8/2026 introduces two key measures for residential rentals:

1. **2% Rent Increase Cap**: The law caps annual rent increases at 2% for eligible properties
2. **Mandatory Extension Option**: Tenants can request up to two additional years of contract extension for leases expiring before December 31, 2027

## Who Is Affected

The measures apply to:
- Residential leases subject to Spain's Urban Leasing Law (LAU)
- Contracts whose annual renewal date falls between March 22, 2026 and December 31, 2027

## Special Rules for Large Landlords

An important distinction exists for larger property portfolios. According to legal analyses, if a landlord owns:
- More than 10 residential properties, OR
- More than 1,500 m² of built residential space

Then the 2% rent increase cap becomes **mandatory** and cannot be waived, even if both tenant and landlord agree to a higher increase.

## How the Extension Process Works

For tenants seeking extensions:
- The tenant must formally request the extension
- The landlord must accept, except in three specific cases:
  1. There is a different agreement between the parties
  2. A new rental contract has already been signed
  3. [Third exception requires verification from official source]

The extension is granted in annual periods, up to a maximum of two years, with all original contract terms and conditions remaining unchanged.

## Congressional Vote Outlook

As of early April 2026, RDL 8/2026 faces a critical parliamentary vote. The law must be validated or repealed by Congress within 30 natural days of its publication (by approximately April 20, 2026).

Based on recent reporting:
- The PP (People's Party) has indicated they will not vote against the decree but may abstain, citing that while it incorporates some of their tax cut proposals, they consider the overall package "insufficient"
- Junts per Catalunya has confirmed they will vote in favor of the anti-crisis decree

If Congress validates the decree, it becomes permanent legislation. If rejected, it is repealed from the date of publication of the rejection.

## What This Means for the Rental Market

With Spain's rental market experiencing a supply crunch (reported as -11% homes for sale year-over-year in recent analyses) and prices at levels not seen since 2006-era levels, RDL 8/2026 represents significant intervention in the rental sector.

The law aims to provide stability for tenants facing potential housing insecurity while imposing constraints on landlords' ability to adjust rents in response to market conditions.

## Looking Ahead

The Congressional vote in the coming days will determine whether these measures become long-term policy or are rolled back. Either outcome will have significant implications for both tenants and landlords across Spain's rental market.
